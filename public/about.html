How this works, and how far to trust it

Where it comes from

Every fact starts in the City of Kansas City's own records: the Legistar item data and the posted minutes and agenda PDFs. Data: City of Kansas City, Missouri, published through Legistar. A job reads them (run by hand for now; a nightly schedule is planned); a model drafts plain-language facts; a second model and code check each fact against the quote it came from. Only facts that pass are shown, and each links to the page of the official document.

Two kinds of content

from the city's records counts, dates, votes outcomes, how often an item was held, topics and links are computed by code from the city's data. AI-written, checked the meeting summaries and short item descriptions are written by a model and shown only after the checks above.

Topic accuracy

I asked a model to tag 300 titles that the rules had not been tuned on, and compared. The model's one main topic was among the rules' topics for the item 86% of the time, and the same as the rules' lead topic 68% of the time. A model's tag is not the truth either, so this measures agreement, not correctness. Per topic: Found is how many of the items a model put under the topic the rules also list there (number sampled in brackets); Lead is how often a model agrees when the rules lead with that topic.

Coverage

200 Council meetings from 2021-01-03 to 2026-10-29 have item records here. 1 has an AI-written summary. Everything else is the city's data, organized.

Known limits

  • The city republishes an agenda as a “Final-Revised” docket after the meeting starts, so “planned” means that final docket, not always the original plan.
  • Keyword topics are imperfect, and an item can be in several. Topics that measured poorly are folded into Other.
  • The way the city records steps has changed over the years; compare across years with care.
  • Votes: this site links to the city's roll calls and does not show or compare how individual council members voted.
  • Models can still be wrong in ways the checks miss. Confirm anything important in the official record.
